Hangul 한글 (The Korean Alphabet)

Hangul is the modern writing system for Korean. Its consonant letters were shaped to echo the position of the mouth, tongue and throat used to pronounce them, and the vowel letters follow their own systematic logic — which is why Hangul is often described as a "featural" alphabet, and sometimes as a syllabic alphabet since letters are grouped into syllable blocks.

Sejong (1397–1450) was the fourth monarch of the Joseon dynasty and is widely regarded as the greatest king in Korean history, remembered above all as the inventor of Hangul.

Hunminjeongeum Haerye 복제본 이미지 자리 (한글 창제 원리 해설서)

In the preface to the Hunminjeongeum, King Sejong explained that because Korean speech differed from Chinese and ordinary people struggled to express themselves in Chinese characters, he had twenty-eight new letters made so that everyone could learn them easily and use them comfortably in daily life.

Dangun 초상화 (채용신, 19–20세기) 이미지 자리

The Legendary Figure

Dangun is the legendary progenitor and first king of the Korean people, founder of Gojoseon, the first Korean kingdom, traditionally dated to 2333 BCE. According to the myth he was the "grandson of heaven" and "son of a bear," born from the union of a divine being and a woman transformed from a bear. The fuller name Dangun Wanggeom combines his title with wanggeom, an old word for "king" or "ruler."

Cultural Significance

Dangun holds a central place in Korean national identity on both sides of the border. In South Korea, National Foundation Day is observed every October 3rd to commemorate the founding of Gojoseon.

Gugak 국악 (Korean Traditional Music)

Korea has produced music for thousands of years, and since the division of Korea in 1945 the North and South have each developed their own styles further. Gugak, literally "national music," covers court music, folk music, poetic song and the religious music of shamanistic and Buddhist tradition.

Pansori 판소리

Pansori is a genre of musical storytelling performed by a single singer and a drummer. The word combines pan, roughly "a gathering" or "a scene," with sori, "sound." A single story (called madang) can run anywhere from three to eight hours, blending song, spoken narration and stylized movement to the beat of a barrel drum (buk) played by an accompanist (gosu). Before "pansori" became the standard term, it was known by names such as taryeong, japga and gukga.

Jjimjilbang 챔질방 ☠

Jjimjilbang are Korean public bathhouses that took off in popularity from the 1990s onward. They're split into gender-separated bathing areas — hot tubs, showers, traditional kiln saunas — plus unisex common areas that can include a snack bar, heated ondol floors for lounging or sleeping, wide-screen TVs, exercise rooms, ice rooms, salt rooms, an internet cafe and karaoke booths.

Many sleeping rooms are built around a theme, with walls finished in wood, minerals, crystal or metal chosen for scent and mood as much as for their traditional medicinal associations. Some jjimjilbang stay open around the clock and double as an overnight option — a place families visit on weekends, and where people who've worked or stayed out late sometimes crash till morning.

Korean Folk Tales

Korean folk tales grew out of older myths and legends, carrying lessons on morality, wisdom and everyday life. Broadly a form of folklore, they're known in Korean as min-tam (민담, 民譚) or min-hwa (민화, 民話), passed down by word of mouth across generations.

The Story of Heungbu and Nolbu

"Heungbu and Nolbu" 애니메이션 embed 자리

Two brothers, greedy Nolbu and kind Heungbu: after Nolbu takes the whole inheritance and drives his brother into poverty, Heungbu nurses an injured swallow back to health and is rewarded with a magic seed. The gourds it grows are full of treasure, making his family wealthy. Nolbu tries to copy him by deliberately hurting a swallow, but his own gourds release goblins, debt collectors and a flood instead. He begs forgiveness and the brothers reconcile — a story that's stayed a byword in Korea for how kindness is rewarded and greed undoes itself.

The Herdsman and the Weaver (견우직녀)

삽화/영상 embed 자리

The Weaver Girl, daughter of the Jade Emperor of Heaven, falls in love with and marries the Herdsman. When the two neglect their duties, the Jade Emperor separates them across the Milky Way, letting them meet only once a year, on the seventh day of the seventh lunar month — the day magpies are said to form a bridge for them, and their tears are said to fall as rain.

The Rabbit and the Tiger

"The Rabbit and the Tiger" 애니메이션 embed 자리

A tiger sets out to catch a rabbit in the forest, but the rabbit plays up how small and helpless it is and talks the tiger into closing its eyes and waiting for a promised meal. While the tiger waits, the rabbit slips away — a classic trickster tale where wit outlasts strength.
